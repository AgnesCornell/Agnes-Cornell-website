About

State capacity, bureaucracy, and democracy

I am an Associate Professor at the Department of Political Science, University of Gothenburg.

My research lies at the intersection of public administration and comparative politics, and centers on three themes. The first concerns the relationship between citizens and the state — how citizens interact with public institutions and their representatives, and what shapes citizens' compliance and trust in the state. The second concerns bureaucracy, both its historical development and its contemporary role in governance. The third concerns the conditions for democratic stability, including the effects of foreign aid on democratization.

My work has been published in journals such as the Journal of Politics, Comparative Political Studies, Governance, and the European Journal of Political Research.

I am co-author of Democratic Stability in an Age of Crisis: Reassessing the Interwar Period (Oxford University Press, 2020, with Jørgen Møller and Svend-Erik Skaaning).

I serve as Director of Studies for the Master's Programmes in Political Science and the Master's Programme in International Administration and Global Governance (IAGG) at the University of Gothenburg.

Research

01

Bureaucracy — Historical and Contemporary

Examining the development of bureaucracy over time — from the historical origins of meritocratic recruitment and administrative reform, to how contemporary bureaucratic structures affect policy implementation, economic growth, political accountability, and democratic quality.

02

Citizen–State Relations

Investigating how citizens interact with public institutions and their representatives — what shapes trust and compliance, how frontline bureaucrats affect citizen behaviour, and how civil society organisations navigate their relationship with the state.

03

Democratic Stability

Studying the conditions under which democracies survive or break down, drawing on comparative and historical evidence.

Ongoing Research Projects

Principal Investigator

Sustaining State Capacity in a Modern State: Citizen–State Relations and Voluntary Compliance in Sweden

This project examines citizen–state relations and voluntary compliance in Sweden. High-capacity states depend on citizens' willingness to comply with state policy, yet Sweden has received many citizens from countries where the state exercises power through coercion. The project uses Swedish register data to study compliance patterns, and tests how different background characteristics, such as country of origin, affect citizens' willingness to engage with the state.

Funder: Swedish Research Council (Vetenskapsrådet) · SEK 4,851,000 Period: 2024–2026 Research team: Rasmus Broms, Andrej Kokkonen, Simon Gren (all University of Gothenburg)
Principal Investigator

Unlawful Influence in Swedish Municipalities: Do Social Ties Matter for Bureaucrats' Vulnerability and Ability to Resist?

Unlawful influence — including threats, harassment, and corruption — poses serious challenges to local government in Sweden. This project investigates how common such attempts are against frontline bureaucrats in municipalities, and whether social ties make them more vulnerable or more resilient. Combining register data, surveys, and interviews, the project generates new knowledge on how unlawful influence operates and how it can be prevented.

Funder: Forte (Swedish Research Council for Health, Working Life and Welfare) · SEK 4,938,000 Period: 2026–2028 Research team: Andrej Kokkonen (University of Gothenburg), Gissur Ó Erlingsson (Linköping University)
Collaborator

When is Civil Society Civil? Institutional Determinants of Claim-Making and Implications for Societal Development

Civil society organisations are often seen as democracy's watchdog — defending rights and holding governments to account. Yet evidence from third-wave democracies also shows that civil society can engage in clientelism, trading political support for targeted benefits. This project examines when and why civil society organisations act in the public interest versus engaging in clientelistic exchanges. It uses surveys with embedded experiments on civil society leaders in Peru, and a field experiment, to shed new light on this dynamic.

Funder: Swedish Research Council (Vetenskapsrådet) · SEK 5,973,060 Period: 2019–2026 PI: Marcia Grimes (University of Gothenburg) · Research team: Agnes Cornell, Paula Muñoz (Universidad del Pacífico)

Publications

Book

Oxford University Press

Democratic Stability in an Age of Crisis: Reassessing the Interwar Period

Oxford University Press · 2020

Agnes Cornell, Jørgen Møller & Svend-Erik Skaaning

A comprehensive reassessment of democratic breakdown and survival during the interwar years (1918–1939). Using a combination of comparative-historical analysis and quantitative methods, the book challenges common analogies with contemporary democratic backsliding and offers new insights into the conditions that made democracies resilient or fragile during a turbulent era.
